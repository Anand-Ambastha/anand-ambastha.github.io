Three positions, one thread: channel models and key-rate analysis for twin-field and satellite QKD.

Jun 2026 – present

Twin-field QKD over turbulent free-space optical channels

Research Intern, IITM C-DOT Samgnya Technologies Foundation (National Quantum Mission), Centre for Applied Research in Electronics, Indian Institute of Technology Delhi. Supervisor: Dr. Neel Kanth Kundu. Co-supervisor: Dr. Ramniwas Meena.

  • Adopted the Curty–Azuma–Lo (CAL) Protocol-3 asymptotic, collective-attack security model as the baseline. Extended it to atmospheric links by replacing the fixed-loss channel with a log-normal turbulence-fluctuating transmittance.
  • Derived a finite-order analytical Hermite-moment expansion in the Gaussian log-transmittance domain, combined with an exact Euler–Stirling transformation back to the physical transmittance domain. It gives the turbulence-averaged X-basis error rate, Z-basis phase-error rate and secret-key rate without repeated Monte Carlo or quadrature at each operating point.
  • Cross-validated two independently derived formulations of the expansion to better than 10⁻⁹ relative agreement across 78 checks, and against direct numerical Gaussian quadrature over a distance and turbulence sweep.
  • Derived a zero-fitted-parameter matched-asymptotic surrogate for the CAL error-rate and key-rate quantities, and validated the closed-form turbulence average against 2 × 10⁵-sample Monte Carlo across eleven operating points.

Jun – Aug 2026

SNS-TF-QKD studies and the QSAFire framework

Research Intern, Scientific Analysis Group (SAG), Defence Research and Development Organisation, New Delhi. Supervisor: Mr. Rajesh Kumar, Scientist ‘E’.

  • Conducted three independent SNS-TF-QKD studies: a physical-layer OptiSystem prototype, a Python classical-processing pipeline, and QSAFire, an independent satellite-link simulation framework. Each is reported and validated separately by design.
  • Built a three-station SNS-TF-QKD prototype in OptiSystem with Alice, Bob and Charlie, 40 km SMF-28 fibre spans and a 10 Gbit/s rate. Window selection, send or not-send, decoy intensity and phase randomisation run as MATLAB co-simulation blocks with bit-index-synchronised CSV logging across all three stations.
  • Built QSAFire, combining J2-perturbation orbital geometry, elevation-dependent slant range, atmospheric loss, Gamma-Gamma turbulence, pointing loss and a fully asymmetric, jointly optimised SNS-TF-QKD key-rate model. Applied it to six Indian ground sites and four candidate links.
  • Identified a plane-wave versus spherical-wave Rytov-variance error in the uplink turbulence model, corrected it with a literature-anchored conversion ratio, and confirmed the fix by running the corrected framework end to end.

Orbital solver and elevation–slant-range formulas validated against Landsat-8/9, Sentinel-2 and published Micius in-orbit data to within 1–3%

Jul 2025 – present

Climatology-aware channel models and QKD simulation

Undergraduate Research Assistant, Bharati Vidyapeeth’s College of Engineering, New Delhi. Supervisor: Dr. Yugnanda Malhotra.

  • Leading undergraduate research in quantum key distribution, free-space optical communication and satellite quantum networking.
  • Developed climatology-aware atmospheric channel models from ERA5 and CAMS reanalysis data for turbulence, attenuation and link availability across Indian quantum-communication corridors.
  • Designed simulation frameworks for BB84, decoy-state BB84 and entanglement-based QKD over terrestrial and satellite FSO channels.
  • Published first-author research in Springer, and contributed to book chapters and conference proceedings on satellite quantum-communication architectures.
