Quantum Networks Under the Lens, Part 0: What does it take to build a global quantum network?

8 June 2026 · 1 min read · Anand Kumar

Over the past month, while working on a couple of research manuscripts in satellite quantum communications, I've found myself coming back to the same question again and again:

What does it actually take to build a global quantum communication network?

When people hear "quantum technology," the conversation usually revolves around quantum computers, algorithms, or cryptography.

But once you start thinking about real-world deployment, an entirely different set of challenges appears.

  • Orbital mechanics.
  • Atmospheric physics.
  • Optical communication.
  • Network scheduling.
  • Constellation design.
  • Operational efficiency.

What surprised me most was how many seemingly straightforward assumptions become much less obvious when physics enters the picture.

  • Does adding more satellites always help?
  • Can geography matter more than hardware?
  • Should networks be optimized for peak performance or long-term stability?

Over the next few weeks, I'll be sharing a short series called:

"Quantum Networks Under the Lens" Five Questions Physics Still Hasn't Fully Answered

These posts are inspired by questions I've been exploring while preparing upcoming manuscripts on satellite Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) and quantum communication networks.

I won't be sharing results or conclusions yet.

Just interesting questions, ideas, and discussions from the journey.

Because sometimes the most valuable part of research isn't the answer...it's learning how to ask better questions.
