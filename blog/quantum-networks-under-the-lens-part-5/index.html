Quantum Networks Under the Lens, Part 5: Can geography create quantum advantage?

13 July 2026 · 1 min read · Anand Kumar

Consider two identical quantum communication systems.

  • Same satellite
  • Same hardware
  • Same protocol
  • Same network architecture

But located in different places.

Should they perform the same?

At first glance, perhaps yes.

But Earth's atmosphere is far from uniform.

Depending on where a ground station is located, photons may encounter very different conditions:

  • Altitude
  • Humidity
  • Aerosol concentration
  • Cloud cover
  • Seasonal weather patterns

All of these factors shape the optical channel through which quantum signals travel.

This leads to a fascinating question:

Can geography itself become a strategic resource for future quantum communication infrastructure?

In other words, could choosing the right location provide a larger advantage than making incremental improvements to the hardware?

It's a question that sits at the intersection of physics, atmospheric science, and network engineering and one I've been exploring while preparing an upcoming research manuscript.

Sometimes the most important system parameter isn't built by engineers.

It's provided by nature.

What do you think?
