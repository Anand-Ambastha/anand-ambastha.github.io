Suppose you're designing a future quantum communication network.

**Would you place your satellites at 300 km or 1200 km altitude?**

At first glance, higher sounds better.

- More coverage
- Longer visibility windows
- Fewer coverage gaps

But physics introduces a catch.

As satellites move farther away, photons have to travel longer distances, and optical losses begin to grow.

Lower orbits can provide:

- Stronger optical links
- Lower path loss
- Higher potential throughput

Higher orbits can provide:

- Larger coverage footprints
- Better availability
- Longer contact times

So which one should we optimize for?
Availability or throughput?

The interesting part is that improving one doesn't necessarily improve the other.

Every design choice seems to solve one problem while creating a new one.

I've been thinking a lot about this tradeoff while working on system-level models for satellite Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) networks.

If you were designing a global quantum communication network from scratch, where would you start?
