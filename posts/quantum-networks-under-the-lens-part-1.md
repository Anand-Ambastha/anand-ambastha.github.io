At first glance, the answer seems obvious.

More satellites should mean more coverage, more connectivity, and ultimately more secure key generation.

Right?

That's what I thought too.

But the deeper I looked into satellite quantum communication networks, the more I realized that coverage and throughput aren't necessarily the same thing.

Adding satellites can increase visibility and reduce coverage gaps.

However, the amount of useful key material generated still depends on the quality of the optical links themselves.

That raises an interesting question:

If we keep adding satellites to a constellation, will throughput continue growing at the same rate?

Or is there a point where coverage keeps improving while the throughput gains become smaller and smaller?

It's a deceptively simple question, but physics rarely rewards simple intuition.

I've been exploring this topic while preparing an upcoming manuscript on large-scale satellite quantum communication networks.

What would your intuition predict?
