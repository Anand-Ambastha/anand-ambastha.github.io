---
title: The theory of QKD is moving faster than its tooling
date: 2026-05-11
tags: [qkd, simulation, open-source, research-tools]
summary: Protocols and proofs are advancing quickly, but the tools to simulate, reproduce and extend them are still fragmented. A note on what is missing.
---

One thing I’ve slowly realized while exploring quantum communication and QKD research is that the theory side of the field is progressing much faster than the tooling ecosystem around it.

We already have:

- Beautiful protocols
- Strong security proofs
- Satellite QKD demonstrations
- Free-space optical research
- Attack models
- Quantum network architectures

But when it comes to actually experimenting, simulating, reproducing, or extending these systems computationally, things still feel very fragmented.

A lot of implementations are: paper-specific, hard to extend, minimally documented, or abandoned after publication.

And honestly, once realistic conditions enter the picture like turbulence, detector imperfections, atmospheric losses, pointing errors, and attack-aware simulations, the gap becomes even more visible.

Sometimes it feels like researchers spend almost as much time rebuilding infrastructure as they do exploring new ideas.

There are good tools in the ecosystem already, and many researchers have built genuinely impressive simulators and frameworks over the years.

But it still feels like there’s room for something more modular, reusable, physics-aware, and easier to build upon collaboratively.

Especially as quantum communication slowly moves from theory-heavy demonstrations toward scalable real-world systems.

Curious to hear thoughts from others working around quantum networks, QKD, photonics, or simulation infrastructure.
