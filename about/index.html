About

I model how far quantum keys can travel through real air.

B.Tech student in Electronics and Communication Engineering in New Delhi, researching quantum key distribution over free-space and satellite links.

Also known as Anand Verma · Anand Ambastha · AV

I am studying Electronics and Communication Engineering at Guru Gobind Singh Indraprastha University in New Delhi. My research asks one question: how far and how fast can a quantum key be shared when the link runs through real atmosphere, from a ground station to a satellite or between two points on the ground?

I work on it from the channel side. At the Indian Institute of Technology Delhi, with Dr. Neel Kanth Kundu and co-supervisor Dr. Ramniwas Meena, I study twin-field QKD over turbulent free-space optical links and derive analytical ways to average the key rate over turbulence, so results that would need repeated Monte Carlo runs can be computed directly. At the Scientific Analysis Group of the Defence Research and Development Organisation, I built QSAFire, a satellite-to-ground SNS-TF-QKD simulator checked against published satellite data, and a three-station fibre prototype in OptiSystem. At Bharati Vidyapeeth’s College of Engineering, I build atmospheric channel models from ERA5 and CAMS reanalysis data for Indian ground stations.

I try to check every result in more than one way, for example an analytical expansion against numerical quadrature and Monte Carlo, and I keep my frameworks as open-source Python on GitHub.

Outside the lab, I am President of BVP Optica, my college’s optics and photonics society, where I organise workshops and research sessions. I also coordinate student research at the Neurogenomo Research Initiative. In 2026 I was selected for the Optica Student Leadership Conference in Rochester, with travel and conference funding from Optica.

If you work on free-space or satellite quantum links, I would like to hear from you. Get in touch.
